# Nexora Care triage prioritization project report

**Experimental output. Not a clinical decision.**

The experiment can estimate recorded urgency labels, but the observed errors and
unvalidated label provenance prevent any claim of safe autonomous triage. The
selected class-weighted LightGBM achieved test macro F1 **0.700**, High recall
**68.4%** and Critical recall **74.4%**. Under-triage remains
**15.5%**, including **9 severe errors**. These results describe
classification against saved reference labels, not clinical accuracy or improved
patient outcomes.

## Business problem and objectives

Nexora Care Flow supports clinics and provider networks with patient records,
scheduling, telemedicine and care coordination. Its chronological request queue
can put an urgent submission behind an earlier routine one. The business question
is whether intake information can support human prioritization review, while an
analytical dashboard helps staff inspect evidence and model errors.

The implementation prepares linked intake/assessment records, explores associations,
tests hypotheses, compares classifiers and imbalance strategies, evaluates a frozen
model, and demonstrates experimental predictions. It learns clinician-assigned
`clinical_urgency` labels ordered Low, Moderate, High, Critical. It neither diagnoses
nor establishes an appropriate waiting time. No operational study has measured queue
benefit, clinician workload or patient outcomes.

Mixed adapted protocols, a local protocol and clinical judgment could encode

protocol-related label noise is a plausible limitation rather than a measured
error rate. `triage_protocol` and every assessment field except the target are

Treat synthetic or limited case-study data as insufficient evidence of clinical
representativeness. The supplied artifacts do not independently establish whether
records are synthetic, how they were sampled, or whether protocol names correspond
to independently applied clinical procedures. Confirm provenance before reuse.

## Hypothesis testing and statistical interpretation

The saved workflow tests 9 primary comparisons and 17
individual-item secondary comparisons at alpha 0.05. Null/alternative statements,
assumptions, exclusion counts, group summaries, statistics and interpretation are in
[the omnibus CSV](../artifacts/hypothesis_test_results.csv). The numeric null concerns
rank/distribution equality; categorical nulls concern independence. No causal claim
follows rejection.

Assumption procedure from the artifact: Group Shapiro (seeded maximum 5000) and median-centred Levene at alpha; non-rejection does not establish assumptions; KW for failures, no median-only claim without common shapes.
Numeric comparisons in these saved results use Kruskal–Wallis because assumptions
fail; full-request independence also fails. Kruskal–Wallis does not repair that
failure. Categorical tests use chi-square, with sparse-table calibration as saved:
Fixed-margin conditional Monte Carlo Pearson statistic, 19999 resamples, seed 42; finite MC resolution and standard error reported. Vital comparisons exclude missing exposure/target pairs separately;
unknown multi-value responses are excluded from presence/absence comparisons.

The case study proposes an ordinal Kruskal–Wallis test for severity. The implementation
instead tests severity as categorical independence with Cramér's V. This is a valid
association question but does not specifically test an ordered trend and departs
from the proposed severity test. ANOVA/Tukey branches exist for suitable assumptions;
they were not forced onto these results.

## Experimental methodology

Only intake information available at request submission is eligible. Assessment
notes, assessor/assessment identifiers, escalation, protocol and assessment time are
excluded. Request/patient IDs and raw timestamps are metadata for grouping/history,
never learned inputs. Cleaning and fixed vocabularies are deterministic; all learned
imputation, encoding, scaling, resampling and weighting are fit inside pipelines.

Numeric features use median imputation and scaling; ordinal fields preserve order;
categoricals use one-hot encoding; fixed multi-hot and availability indicators pass
through. Class balancing is computed inside each fit. Optional TF-IDF is disabled.
No model was selected using the test outcomes.

### Split and repeated-patient handling

Patients are assigned by their first labelled-request chronological window. Later
requests from an earlier-window patient are purged rather than moved into a later
split. This enforces patient disjointness and strict retained chronology, while
reducing later cohorts and shifting the estimand toward new labelled patients.

development preferences, not clinician-elicited utilities or an approved safety
standard. The selected model keeps uncalibrated probabilities and argmax decisions;
the cost-sensitive logistic alternative demonstrates a different under/over-triage
trade-off rather than an objectively inferior clinical policy.

Structural leakage checks found 0 aggregate suspicious-score flags;
prohibited transformed predictors and exact target copies were absent in the saved
audit. This does not rule out label-construction circularity or earlier exploratory
exposure. Investigate suspicious separation before interpreting feature importance
as independent confirmation.

## Under-triage and over-triage

Under-triage predicts a lower urgency rank than the reference label; over-triage
predicts a higher rank. Overall under-triage is 166/1,074 (15.5%);
over-triage is 157/1,074 (14.6%). Rates below use each true class's
support, so they must not be confused with the overall denominator.

| True class | n | Under n | Under rate | Over n | Over rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low | 380 | 0 | 0.0% | 73 | 19.2% |
| Moderate | 380 | 98 | 25.8% | 58 | 15.3% |
| High | 228 | 46 | 20.2% | 26 | 11.4% |
| Critical | 86 | 22 | 25.6% | 0 | 0.0% |

Severe under-triage is Critical→Low/Moderate or High→Low:
**9 cases (0.8% of all test requests; 2.9% of true
High/Critical requests)**. Breakdown: Critical to Low: 0; Critical to Moderate: 3; High to Low: 6.
The requested broader dashboard review group, Critical→any lower class plus
High→Low/Moderate, contains 68 cases. This includes Critical→High
and High→Moderate, which are not in the narrower severe definition.

Errors show that recognizing higher-urgency labels remains incomplete. Over-triage
could burden clinician capacity; under-triage could delay review if a queue followed
these outputs. Neither operational consequence was measured. Do not convert these
error rates into patient-harm estimates. See [error exports](../artifacts/under_over_triage.csv)
and the dedicated dashboard review page.

Multi-hot red-flag/comorbidity items, counts and unknown/none indicators are grouped
by summing signed contributions per request before averaging absolute values.
This preserves cancellation instead of inflating a family by summing absolute
components. Systolic and diastolic pressures remain separate, as do availability
indicators. Per-class summaries and individual waterfalls explain raw class margins,
not probability changes, causes or treatment recommendations.

Severity and red flags may participate in the creation of the target labels. Their
prominence is not proof of leakage by itself, but the separated red-flag outcome
and heterogeneous protocols warrant source-label review. Full notes and figures:
[explainability](../artifacts/explainability/explainability_notes.md),

The saved full raw-input pipeline and separate preprocessing pipeline are
`models/triage_model.joblib` and `models/preprocessing_pipeline.joblib`. The schema
includes model version/configuration, raw input requirements, urgency mapping and
probability order. Reload validation returned probability shape [1, 4]
and confirmed separate-preprocessing parity and normalization on a synthetic row.
Only trusted, compatible Joblib artifacts should be loaded.

[The test prediction table](../artifacts/model_predictions.csv) contains prediction
and request IDs, predicted class, a non-clinical index, class probabilities, model
version and UTC prediction timestamp. Formula: `100 * (P(Moderate) + 2*P(High) + 3*P(Critical)) / 3`.

sketch uses the reversed naming order. The semantic mapping is documented in

## Limitations and clinical safety

The main evidence limits are synthetic/limited case-study provenance, uncertain
self-reported units and measurements, potential inconsistent labels across the
5 protocols, restricted age/demographic coverage, missingness and
unlabelled-request selection. Repeated patients invalidate ordinary request-level
independence. Single-request sensitivity removes repeats but not confounding,
site/clinician clustering or selection bias. Correlated features and deterministic
label-construction rules can inflate apparently persuasive patterns.

Adaptive validation reuse, conditional bootstrap stability, fractional encoded
SMOTE values and sparse calibration bins constrain modelling conclusions. The
patient/time purge changes the population evaluated. The test cohort was reserved
for model fitting/selection but was not fully blinded to earlier EDA and statistical
analysis. No external/prospective clinical validation, protocol agreement audit,
subgroup fairness assurance, deployment study or patient-outcome benefit has been
established. Probabilities are not validated clinical risk estimates.

Qualified professionals must retain responsibility. Model outputs must not override
warning signs, clinical judgment or existing emergency escalation procedures. A low
output must never be used to cancel a red flag or delay appropriate review. Any
future clinical trial or operational use requires clinician-led validation,
protocol/label adjudication, subgroup testing, realistic calibration and workload
assessment, approved escalation/fallback pathways, privacy/security controls and
ongoing monitoring under suitable governance. These are prerequisites, not completed
project deliverables.

Production telemedicine software, APIs, containerization, cloud/dashboard deployment,
live queue integration, automated retraining and autonomous clinical decisions are
explicitly out of scope in the case study. The current dashboard is an experimental
analytical demonstration; it is not a production triage workflow.
